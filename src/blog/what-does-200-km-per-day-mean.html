"200 km per day" means the daily rate includes up to 200 kilometres of driving for each day of the rental. If you drive more, the extra kilometres are charged at a per-km rate. Many companies add the allowance up over the whole rental, so a three-day booking with 200 km per day gives you 600 km to use however you like. Ask how your rental company counts it.

How the extra-kilometre charge works

Say you book a car for three days with 200 km per day included. The allowance is 600 km. If you drive 700 km in total, you pay for the 100 km over the allowance at the per-km rate in your agreement. If you drive 450 km, you pay the normal daily rate and nothing extra.

The odometer reading is noted when you collect the car and again when you return it, so take a photo of both.

Unlimited kilometres or a daily limit?

A daily limit usually keeps the base rate lower, which suits city use and short trips. Unlimited kilometres, or a rate built for out-of-city travel, suits long drives. Work out roughly how far you'll go, then compare the total cost of each option.

With a driver: hours as well as kilometres

When you rent a car with a driver, the price usually depends on the hours and the route as well as the distance. Long days can add overtime, and trips outside the twin cities add an allowance for the driver.
