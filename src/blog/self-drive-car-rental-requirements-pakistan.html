To rent a self-drive car in Pakistan you normally need your original CNIC (or passport for foreign nationals), a valid driving licence and a refundable security deposit. Below is what rental companies check, what to look at before you drive away, and the road rules that catch visitors out in Islamabad.

Documents you need

  • Pakistani citizens: your original CNIC, or NICOP if you live abroad.
  • Foreign nationals: your passport and the driving licence from your home country. An International Driving Permit alongside it is the safest option.
  • A valid driving licence for cars. Bring the original, not a photo.
  • A phone number the company can reach you on during the rental. Some companies also ask for a local address or a reference.

The security deposit

Self-drive rentals need a refundable security deposit. The amount depends on the car. It covers fuel shortfalls, traffic fines and minor damage, and it is returned when the car comes back on time and in the same condition. Ask for the amount in writing with your quote.

Check the car before you drive away

  • Walk around the car with the company's staff and photograph or video every scratch and dent, including the roof and bumpers.
  • Note the fuel level and the odometer reading.
  • Check the tyres, the spare wheel and the jack.
  • Make sure the car's registration papers are in the car.
  • Test the air conditioning, lights and wipers.
  • If you plan to use the motorway, check the car has an M-Tag with enough balance for the tolls.

Fuel and mileage

Ask how fuel works. Common policies are "full to full" (collect it full, return it full) and "same to same" (return it with the level you collected it at). Also ask whether there is a daily mileage limit and what extra kilometres cost; our guide to what "200 km per day" means explains how that works.

Road rules to know in Islamabad

  • Pakistan drives on the left.
  • Wear your seat belt. Islamabad Traffic Police fine drivers who don't.
  • Don't use your phone while driving.
  • Islamabad's Safe City cameras are used to issue e-challans for violations such as speeding and running red lights. Fines raised during your rental are your responsibility.
  • On the motorways the speed limit for cars is 120 km/h, enforced by the National Highways & Motorway Police.
  • In winter, roads to Murree and the Galiyat can be icy or closed after snowfall. Check conditions before you go.

If something goes wrong

Call the rental company first; they will tell you what to do and where to take the car. In an emergency, call the police on 15, or the motorway police on 130 if you are on a motorway. Photograph the scene and any damage, and don't agree to repairs without the rental company's approval.
